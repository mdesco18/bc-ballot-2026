How the record notes work

Some names carry a collapsed Record or Promises note: dated, linked entries, not a score. There is deliberately no ranking and no index.

Coverage follows scrutiny, not conduct. A premier or a cabinet minister is examined daily; a first-term backbencher rarely is. A score would make the unexamined person look clean, which inverts what a voter wants to know. No note means nothing was found or nobody looked, and those are not the same thing. Where a person was searched and nothing turned up, the page says so.

Every entry carries two tags

The first says what kind of thing it is.

  • Financial interest — self-dealing or benefit.
  • Election finance — spending and donation rules.
  • Conduct in office — Conflict of Interest Commissioner, Speaker and caucus matters.
  • Record — verifiable facts about performance, such as votes, bills, expenses and party changes.
  • Cleared — investigations that ended in exoneration, included so the page is not only bad news.

The second says how well established it is.

  • Finding — a body with jurisdiction ruled: the Conflict of Interest Commissioner, a court, Elections BC, the Legislature.
  • Filed — a complaint or petition exists and was not decided against the person.
  • Reported — substantive journalism from a named outlet, linked.

Rumour, social media and opinion columns are excluded, and so is anything that cannot be linked.

Promises

For sitting MLAs: whether a commitment they personally made in 2024, or own as the responsible minister, was kept, partially kept, broken or reversed. The party platform as a whole is not charged to every MLA who ran under it. For new platforms, no judgment on whether a plan is a good idea, only two checkable flags: outside the province's power and costing questioned.

What this page stores

This page never learns who you plan to vote for. Nothing you do here leaves your device. There is no account, no sign-in, no cookies, no analytics and no form that submits anywhere. Nobody, including the person who shared this link with you, can see which candidates you marked.

Your shortlist is written to your own browser's local storage. It survives closing the tab, but never syncs: marks made on your phone will not appear on your laptop, and clearing your browser data erases them. Everyone you send the link to gets their own empty shortlist.

Print your shortlist with the button at the top of the candidate list. Printing happens on your own machine.

How BC provincial elections work

British Columbia elects 93 Members of the Legislative Assembly, one per electoral district. You vote once, for one candidate in your own district, and the candidate with the most votes wins the seat. The party that can hold the confidence of the legislature forms government; 47 seats is a majority.

Your district is set by your home address. Several cities in this guide are split across two to twelve districts, and two districts cross a city line: West Vancouver-Capilano takes in part of the District of North Vancouver, and Oak Bay-Gordon Head part of the City of Victoria.

Voting places, advance voting dates and registration are run by Elections BC. Use My District to find your district and Where to Vote to find a voting place. To register or update your registration, use online voter registration.

Why this exists

This is the provincial companion to the local elections guide, built the same way: every name in one place, grouped by party, with a shortlist you can print. Nothing you do on this page is collected or sent anywhere.

This guide covers 26 of the 93 districts. For every district and candidate in the province, with 2024 results and how each MLA voted in the Legislature, see bcballot.ca, an independent site not connected to this one. It also has guides on how and where to vote. It is listed here as a resource only: this guide does not verify or endorse its content, so check names and facts against Elections BC.
